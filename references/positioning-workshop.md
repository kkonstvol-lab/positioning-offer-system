# Positioning workshop

Goal: define a sharp, honest market angle before building the offer.

## Core positioning questions
- What market angle should the agency lead with?
- Which buyer is the landing really for?
- What painful or expensive problem do they need solved?
- What concrete result do they want?
- What method or working style makes this agency more useful than alternatives?
- Why choose this instead of a freelancer, in-house hire, or generic agency?

## Forced-choice questions (use real competitor names from research)
After competitive research, present these as binary or 3-option forced choices. No "all of the above."

**Q1 — Customer test:**
> Your ideal client is evaluating you against [COMPETITOR A] and [COMPETITOR B]. They can only pick one. Why do they pick you?
> Rules: don't say "price", don't say "quality", don't say "customer service."

**Q2 — Honest gap:**
> What is the single biggest reason an ideal client says NO today?
> Be specific. Not "we're early stage."

**Q3 — Core bet:**
> You can only invest in ONE of these directions for the next 6 months. Which one?
> (Generate 3-4 options based on competitive white space)

**Q4 — Market scope:**
> Which market are you actually in? Pick one.
> (Generate options from narrow to broad based on the agency's services)

The pain of choosing is the exercise. Force a decision.

## Generate 2-3 positioning angles only if direction is fuzzy
Compare each on:
- clarity (does a skeptic understand it in 5 seconds?)
- distinctiveness (could 10 other agencies say the same?)
- believability (is there proof to back it?)
- sales usefulness (does it help close deals?)
- landing translatability (can it become a hero section?)

Choose ONE primary angle. No compromise blobs.

## Positioning statement formula
> We help [specific client] get [valuable result] through [distinctive mechanism], unlike [common alternative].

- Under 30 words
- No unverifiable adjectives (no "innovative", "cutting-edge", "world-class")
- Claims are either true today or explicitly marked as bets

## Output
- Market angle
- Target audience statement (who + size + pain)
- Problem statement
- Differentiated mechanism
- One-sentence positioning statement (≤30 words)
