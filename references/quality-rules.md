# Quality rules

Apply to all outputs: positioning statement, UTP, offer, landing copy.

## Hard constraints
- No sentence over 20 words
- Positioning statement: under 30 words
- No unverifiable adjectives: "innovative", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "unique", "best-in-class", "leading"
- Every claim is either:
  - (a) true and provable today, or
  - (b) explicitly marked as a bet ("By Q3, we plan to...")
- No passive voice in headlines or CTAs
- No "we are proud to..." or "we are pleased to..."

## Smell test
If any of these are true, rewrite:
- A competitor could say the exact same sentence
- The claim requires "trust us" to believe
- The mechanism is described as "proprietary" without explanation
- The result is described as "results" without specifics
- The audience is described as "any business" or "companies of all sizes"

## Anti-patterns (never do these)
- Generate positioning without competitive context
- Accept the first draft as final
- Use branding language instead of commercial specifics
- Answer "who is the client" with a category instead of a person
- Describe the offer without naming what is actually delivered
- Promise a result without a mechanism
- Write a hero headline that starts with "We"

## Self-check before delivering output
Ask yourself:
1. Would a skeptical CFO read this and want to know more?
2. Can I point to proof for every claim?
3. Is the audience specific enough to reject some readers?
4. Is the mechanism visible (not just "our approach")?
5. Does the CTA tell the reader exactly what happens next?

If any answer is NO → fix before delivering.
